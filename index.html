Cascade failure root-cause triage

Enter one cycle's developmental cascade. Each transition is tested against its benchmark (exact binomial), gated for statistical power (minimum-sample gate), and read across the cascade with adapted Westgard rules. Output is a triage hypothesis — A) biological, B) procedural, or C) non-attributable — not a diagnosis.

Benchmarks & expected rates (edit to your validated lab values)
Core transitions (maturation / fertilization / cleavage / blastulation) use the Vienna competency values 75 / 60 / 95 / 40%, applied via Van Der Kelen et al. 2026; cleavage and blastulation both use 2PN as denominator (Vienna convention). Extended transitions use the Vienna Consensus (2017): oocyte recovery is a Vienna RI (80–95%; reflects stimulation/retrieval, not laboratory culture), implantation uses the Vienna blastocyst-stage competency ≥35% (benchmark ≥60%; multifactorial — uterine receptivity, transfer policy), and the usable-blastocyst rate (usable blastocysts / 2PN) uses a literature-weighted competency of ~40% (Chaillot 2024 and Hammond & Morbeck 2019 converge at ~40–41%; Datta 2025, n=14,156, reports a broader utilisation rate nadir ~45%) — not a consensus value, so confirm against your own baseline. Minimum-sample gates are OZEMA-derived for the four core transitions; for the extended transitions, and whenever you edit a benchmark, the gate is recomputed from the same closed form, nmin = ⌈ln .05 / ln(1 − p₀)⌉ — which reproduces the four published OZEMA minima exactly at the default benchmarks. Implantation uses the Vienna competency (≥35%) as its operational default rather than the aspirational benchmark (≥60%). All values are editable to your validated baseline.
threshold in use: P < .05
Up to seven transitions are tested per cycle. In simulation, an uncorrected test across the four core transitions alone gave a per-cycle false-alarm rate of 7.4% against a nominal 5% — roughly one flagged cycle in thirteen. A Bonferroni correction reduced this to 1.3%, and requiring a systemic contiguous pattern to 0.06%. Testing the extended transitions as well inflates it further. Choose deliberately: the uncorrected rule is a screen, not a decision rule.

Developmental cascade

Resolve biological vs procedural — concurrent controls + patient history

The single-cycle verdict above is a weak discriminator. Concurrent sibling-batch controls are the strong one: for each flagged stage, enter how many concurrent cycles (same session / incubator / media lot / operator, different patients) were also low there, and whether the block recurred in this patient. Fixed threshold: a stage is “shared” when ≥50% of concurrent cycles are also low.

κ = (number of the other concurrent cycles also low at this stage) ÷ (number of concurrent cycles)

2×2 logic — shared (κ ≥ 0.5) & not recurrent → procedural (B); recurrent & not shared → biological (A); shared & recurrent → both; neither → still ambiguous. Needs ≥2 concurrent cycles. The 0.5 cut-off is a pragmatic operating point chosen for this tool; the simulation evaluated κ as a continuous discriminator (AUC 0.93) and did not derive an optimal threshold. Companions must be biologically independent — different patients, same session, equipment, consumable lots and operator. For a stage outside the laboratory (retrieval, implantation) a shared deviation implicates the clinical process (trigger, aspiration, transfer), not the laboratory.

How attribution is decided

A stage is read only if its denominator meets the OZEMA minimum sample (else → C non-attributable: under-powered). With adequate power, a rate at/above benchmark passes; a rate below benchmark that is not significant on the one-sided exact binomial test (P≥0.05) → C non-attributable: within chance. A significant drop is a real failure: if it is isolated to one stage, biology and that step's technique are co-primary and genuinely ambiguous in a single cycle → the tool shows both and the disambiguating evidence; if flagged stages are contiguous / systemic (Westgard 2₂ₛ / 4₁ₛ pattern), a procedural-environmental cause is prioritised. A single cycle can rarely separate biology from process on its own — concurrent sibling-batch cycles, the patient's cross-cycle history, and QC logs remain the confirmatory step.

Adapted Westgard rules

Stage transition ratios are converted to z-scores against benchmark (binomial SD) and read as an ordered series. This is a heuristic adaptation of Westgard multirules to the developmental cascade, not analytical-run QC on a control material; the stages are not independent replicate measurements, so treat fired rules as pattern flags, not formal control violations.

Not every underperforming cycle is the laboratory's

The modelled cascade begins downstream of decisions taken elsewhere. Stimulation protocol and dose, trigger timing and adequacy, the interval to retrieval, completeness of aspiration, and the sperm sample presented for insemination all constrain the material on which the laboratory can act, and any of them can depress a downstream proportion without a single process deviation having occurred. Two stages are therefore marked clinical and are excluded from the systemic/contiguity determination and from the Westgard strip: retrieval efficiency (a Vienna reference indicator reflecting ovarian response, trigger and aspiration) and implantation (multifactorial: embryo, endometrium, transfer policy). A flag there routes to the clinical pathway, not to a laboratory investigation. Where the eventual verdict is biological, the correct response is a structured clinical and, where indicated, genetic investigation — not a search for a culpable step or a culpable person. Where it is procedural, the target is the system rather than the individual: blame directed at operators obscures the latent conditions that permitted the error and suppresses the reporting on which detection depends.

Notes & caveats

Benchmarks should be your laboratory's own validated values; imported off-the-shelf values risk systematic misclassification. Single transfers are statistically uninformative. This tool is a hypothesis-generating triage aid; it does not diagnose a patient or certify a process, and a clean concurrent-control signal localises an investigation rather than proving causation. It has been evaluated by simulation only — prospective clinical validation has not been performed. A triage rule of this kind has value only inside a functioning quality-management system: documented procedures, validated equipment and consumables, competency assessment, internal quality control on each batch, external quality assessment, and periodic re-derivation of benchmarks. Continuous quality control and quality assurance are not the backdrop against which root-cause analysis is performed; they are what make it possible at all.
